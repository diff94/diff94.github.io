I'm **Po-Ya Angela Wang** (王伯雅  most people just say **Amber**), a computational linguist who studies how people and machines learn to talk with each other, not merely at each other.

I completed my Ph.D. in Linguistics at **National Taiwan University** (2026), where my dissertation put pairs of multimodal LLM agents into a classic tangram referential game and asked a simple question with a complicated answer: *when models talk to each other, do they coordinate the way humans do?* (Spoiler: they ace the task, but the way they get there is very different, and very revealing.) The dissertation received an **Honorable Mention** for the Ph.D. Dissertation Award of the **Linguistic Society of Taiwan** (2026).

Along the way I spent a year at the Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics (Multimodal Language Department) as a visiting PhD and later an invited visiting scholar. I now serve as **Deputy Executive Director of the Co-Intelligence Humanities AI (CHAI) Future Lab** at NTU's College of Liberal Arts : a "humanities compute center" where we translate research ideas into working systems.

Underneath the research life, I have two bachelor's degrees (English *and* Chinese, NTNU), certified in Teaching Chinese as a Second Language and secondary education, 7+ years of university teaching, and a stubborn belief that AI in the classroom should start from language, culture, and human judgment not just from the technology.
